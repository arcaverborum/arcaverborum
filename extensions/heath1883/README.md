# Heath 1883: Canichana vocabulary

**Source.** Heath, Edwin R. 1883. "Dialects of Bolivian Indians: a philological
contribution from material gathered during three years residence in the Department
of Beni, in Bolivia." *The Kansas City Review of Science and Industry* 6(12) (April
1883): 679–687. The comparative vocabulary is printed sideways on pp. 683–687. It
gives English glosses (the Smithsonian 211-word list) with Canichana, Cayuába,
Mobima, Maropa, Mosetena, Pacavara and Tacana columns. **Only the Canichana column
is transcribed here.**

**Scan.** https://archive.org/details/sim_kansas-city-review_1883-04_6_12
(direct PDF: https://archive.org/download/sim_kansas-city-review_1883-04_6_12/sim_kansas-city-review_1883-04_6_12.pdf).
The PDF has sha256 `285bb2a38943c6ae4b08483422f8410a41596a5873aa7668593deca56f37efcd`
and is 16,019,575 bytes. PDF pages 8–12 (0-based) are printed pp. 683–687.
`pages/` holds 150-dpi renderings of pp. 682–687, rotated upright: p. 682 has the
statement on the Canichana, and pp. 683–687 are the table.

**Who and how.** The forms were transcribed by an AI agent from page images on
2026-11-04 and verified by a second, independent reading. The second reader read
every Canichana cell from the images, given only the English row skeleton and not the
first transcription. Every disagreement was settled against the image (see VERIFY.md).

**What Heath says about the Canichana** (p. 682): "The Canichana was corrected by Don
Hugo Boger, a German and fine linguist, long resident there." Under the table (p. 687)
he gives the reading rules: "In all these idioms the Spanish rules of pronunciation are
followed: A-áh; E-a; I (or Y alone -ēē—o-ō; U-uin, you; J-h, (h is silent or
scarcely perceptible); Que-ka; Qui-kēē; Gue-ga; Guí-gēē; Ll when not divided as ll in
William; Ñ the sound of "on" in onion." The spelling is therefore Spanish-based
orthography with syllables separated by hyphens. It is **not IPA**, so `Segments` is
empty. A reviewed orthographic profile would be a later step.

## Conventions

- `Value` is the cell exactly as printed (NFC), including the final full stop and any
  parenthesised remark. `Form` is the same with the final full stop removed, the
  editorial overrun bracket `[` removed, and the two printed remarks `(casique)`
  (Chief) and `(German)` (Earth—Land) moved to `Comment`. Hyphens, spaces, accents,
  capitals (e.g. `Ni-Khuj`) and a macron (`Eu-rī-ya`) are kept exactly.
- Overrun lines (`[bus-u-li.`, `[abh.`, `[vi-u-li`, `[li.`) are joined to the form they
  complete; `Comment` says where each overrun was printed. **My elder sister** is
  assigned by layout: its overrun `[abh.` stands on its own line, but the stem
  `Eu-cau-la-mat-an-` stands one line lower, beside "My younger". Both readers agreed,
  and the `-mat-` stem parallels "my elder brother" `E-mat-to-ui`.
- `Parameter.Name` is Heath's English gloss as printed, without the final full stop.
  The pronoun row printed **"T."** is evidently a misprint for "I."; the Name is kept as
  printed and the row is mapped to I.
- `Source` is `heath1883_Heath1883[page]`.
- Loans: `I-púb-lo` (Town, village) is marked `Loan = true` because of its obvious
  identity with Spanish *pueblo*. No other loan is asserted.
- Concepticon: 154 of the 165 glosses are mapped. The kin rows "said by Son/Daughter/
  Father/Mother" are mapped to the exact concepts with ego sex (FATHER (OF A MAN),
  SON OF WOMAN, …). Ambiguous or composite glosses are left unmapped: Day, Kettle,
  Prairie, Squash, Fox, Pigeon, Many—much, To Lie, Town, village, An Indian, and Light
  green.

## Exclusions

- 29 English rows have an **empty Canichana cell** and produce no form: Warrior,
  Skin-lodge, Axe—hatchet, Ice, Hill—Mountain, Bear, Wolf, Guinea Pig, Tortoise,
  Mosquito, and the 19 numerals Four to One Thousand.
- There are no unreadable cells, no phrases, and no proper names. 194 English rows give
  165 forms.

## Known issues

- The print is worn. 16 rows carry an `unclear:` comment. Several separators print as
  raised dots and are read as worn hyphens. Three ŭ readings (`daŭ`, `hŭti`, `ëŭ`) show
  a curved mark unlike the clear acutes elsewhere, and might be damaged acutes.
- Identity: Canichana (`cani1243`), the isolate of the Mamoré, Beni. Créqui-Montfort &
  Rivet 1913 (extension `cmrivet1913`) reprint these 165 Heath words in their own
  respelling as source "(2)". Those are **the same data, not an independent
  confirmation**. Earlier Arca rows for Canichana (northperulex) may also derive in part
  from Heath via Crevels 2012, so check them before counting them as independent.
