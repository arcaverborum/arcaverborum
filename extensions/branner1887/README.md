## Branner's notation (from pp. 329–330)

"On account of its simplicity, I have used the Portuguese spelling wherever it is possible." He notes sounds like German *ch*, English *th*, *u*, *w*, *h*, and for *Ichitá* "Ich in this word has the sound of the German Ich." He adds diacritics: acute accents, breves (ŭ, ŏ, ĕ, ĭ), macrons (ā, ē, ī, ō, ū) and a separate raised stress tick printed **above** a breve or macron. The stacked mark is encoded as base letter + breve/macron + combining acute (e.g. *Tskŭ́h*, *Ṓyĕ*, *D'hṓ hṓ*). Nothing here is IPA; `Segments` is empty.

## Conventions

* `Value` = `Form` = the form as printed (no alternatives occur). The English gloss is printed by Branner; parameter `Name` = printed gloss | English normalisation.

## Identity

Carnijó = Fulniô = Yaathe/Iatê (fuln1247). Earliest written record of the language; an amateur's one-hour notes from a single speaker, so treat as written-only evidence beside the existing GLED rows.
